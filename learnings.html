01The outer wrapper (Realtime-beat-tracking, 2018)

A pyaudio recorder process appends 20 ms int16 chunks into a giant multiprocessing.Array('d', samp_rate*60*3) shared buffer (audio_interface_control.py:51-68). An "operation center" (main.py:225-336) round-robins 10-second audio windows to worker processes (one per physical core minus one, main.py:78-79). Each worker (single_worker.py:60-77) runs madmom's RNNDownBeatProcessor → DBNDownBeatTrackingProcessor to get beat times + downbeat positions. Predictions are reconciled across overlapping workers (beat_tracking_util.py:90-110).

The novelty is the parallel scheduling, not the DSP. For us this is mostly an anti-reference.

Avoid copying from the outer wrapper:
  • Multiprocessing + giant multiprocessing.Array (main.py:48-53, 97-111). Wrong model — we use threads + lock-guarded stores.
  • np.hstack into an ever-growing array inside the audio callback path (audio_interface_control.py:96-129) — exactly the allocation-in-callback antipattern.
  • Deprecated APIs: np.fromstring, librosa.output.write_wav, Python 2 (audio_interface_control.py:23, 49, 55).
  • 10-second decision latency (main.py:76). Incompatible with realtime visuals.
  • time.sleep-driven busy-wait coordination + random worker assignment (main.py:69, 156, 175, 226, 270).
  • np.median/np.mean on length-3/length-8 arrays in inner loops (beat_tracking_util.py:66, 79-86) — numpy-ufunc-overhead trap we already avoid by unrolling.

02Onset Detection Functions — onsets.py

All ODFs operate on a magnitude spectrogram S[t, k] (typically log-filtered onto 24 bands/octave). Method registry: onsets.py:645-649.

ODFFormulaSourceCostNotes
Spectral Flux SF[t] = Σ_k max(0, S[t,k] − S[t−τ,k]) onsets.py:163-193 cheap Canonical baseline. L1 of half-wave-rectified spectral diff.
Spectral Diff SD[t] = Σ_k max(0, S[t,k] − S[t−τ,k])² onsets.py:130-160 cheap L2 variant. Slightly peakier.
SuperFlux ⭐ SuF[t] = Σ_k max(0, S[t,k] − maxj∈[k−M,k+M] S[t−τ,j]) onsets.py:196-240 cheap SF with a frequency-axis max-filter on the previous frame. Suppresses vibrato. diff_max_bins=3 ⇒ M=1.
ComplexFlux SuperFlux × local group-delay mask onsets.py:245-317 med Needs phase. Best classical ODF for sustained-pitch material.
HFC HFC[t] = mean_k (k · S[t,k]) onsets.py:102-127 trivial Great for hats/snares, terrible for kicks.
Modified KL MKL[t] = mean_k log(1 + S[t,k]/(S[t−τ,k]+ε)) onsets.py:320-362 cheap Relative (not absolute) flux — invariant to level. Robust under compression.
Phase Dev / Cplx Domain phase-based variants onsets.py:365-581 med Niche. SuperFlux family already covers the realtime use case.
What to take: Our current detector runs on 3 post-processed L/M/H scalars per block — effectively a 3-bin SF on bands we've already tilted, smoothed, and AutoScaled. Moving to per-bin SuperFlux on the FFTPostProcessor's log-spaced bins at hop rate (~100 Hz) is the single most impactful upgrade available: it's the canonical realtime ODF, we already have the right data structure (log-spaced log-mag bins), and the math is three ops per hop: 
# prev_max ← max-filter of previous bins across ±1 bin (reflect)
np.maximum(prev[:-2], prev[1:-1], out=prev_max[1:-1])
np.maximum(prev_max[1:-1], prev[2:], out=prev_max[1:-1])
# flux ← Σ max(0, cur − prev_max)
np.subtract(cur, prev_max, out=diff)
np.maximum(diff, 0.0, out=diff)
flux = diff.sum()
Alloc-free, microseconds per hop. SuperFlux is monotonically related to our fast-minus-slow envelope but operates pre-AutoScale, so the spectral contour of the transient is preserved instead of being smashed into 3 numbers. Kick / snare / hat become reliably separable even when L/M/H edges aren't ideal for the genre.

03Peak-picking — peak_picking at onsets.py:874-963

Three-stage filter on a 1D activation a[t]:

  1. Smooth: optional Hamming convolution (off by default for NN activations).
  2. Adaptive-threshold gate: m[t] = uniform_filter(a, size=pre_avg+post_avg+1, origin=...); keep a[t] ≥ m[t] + threshold.
  3. Local-max gate: M[t] = maximum_filter(detections, size=pre_max+post_max+1, ...); keep detections[t] == M[t].
  4. Combine within ±combine seconds (default 30 ms, onsets.py:1039).

Online wrapper OnsetPeakPickingProcessor (onsets.py:966-1107) sets post_*=0, smooth=0, and combines with 'left' so the first of a near-duplicate cluster is reported.

For SF-style ODFs the paper-tuned values are pre_avg≈100ms / post_avg≈70ms / pre_max≈30ms / post_max≈30ms / threshold≈1.25 (Böck 2012). Defaults at onsets.py:1032-1041.

Comparing to our Schmitt-trigger: Our existing detector is roughly the online equivalent of stages 2+3 collapsed — slow envelope ≈ pre_avg, sensitivity × slow ≈ threshold, refractory ≈ combine. The piece we lack is an explicit local-maximum check: our nov > thr_high fires on the first frame above threshold, not the peak. For sharp transients that's 1-2 frames of jitter; for slow swells it can fire ~50 ms early.

Cheap fix: track nov_prev[i] and fire only on the falling edge (state == armed AND nov[i] < nov_prev[i]) — we cross the threshold to arm, then fire on the frame after the peak. One extra scalar comparison per band per block, removes onset-time jitter.

04Tempo estimation — tempo.py

Comb-filter method (default, online-friendly)

For each delay τ ∈ [min_τ, max_τ] (in frames), run a backward IIR comb on the activation a[n]: 

y_τ[n] = a[n] + α · y_τ[n − τ]     # α = ALPHA = 0.79 (tempo.py:22)

Online variant (tempo.py:375-418): maintain a 2D buffer _comb_buffer[max_τ+1, n_taus], update with y_n = act + α · _comb_buffer[-intervals], argmax-pool into a histogram over the last HIST_BUFFER = 10 s (tempo.py:27).

For our use case: ~70 IIRs at 100 Hz ≈ 7 kops. Trivial. No FFT, no autocorrelation, no NN.

ACF method (alternative)

bins[τ] = Σ_n a[n] · a[n − τ] (tempo.py:62-106). Equivalent to a comb with α=0. Simpler but less robust at noisy activations — the resonant comb integrates evidence in phase, ACF doesn't.

Picking the tempo (detect_tempo, tempo.py:198-255)

  1. Quadratic-interp histogram for sub-bin resolution.
  2. bpm = 60·fps/τ.
  3. scipy.signal.argrelmax(bins, mode='wrap') — local maxima with edge wrap.
  4. Smoothing: act_smooth=0.14 s Hamming on activation; hist_smooth=9 bins on the histogram.
  5. Range: MIN_BPM=40, MAX_BPM=250.

No log-Gaussian prior, no common-tempo bias, no octave-folding. The histogram is the prior — the α=0.79 leak naturally biases comb response toward the fundamental, not its multiples. Typical failure mode is half-tempo doubling on dense music, not octave errors.

Why this replaces our median-of-12-IOIs: Our IOI estimator is brittle to missed or spurious onsets — a single missed kick injects a 2× IOI that pollutes the median window for the next 12 onsets (~6 seconds at 120 BPM). The comb integrates evidence in phase: a missed onset just dips the right τ's resonance for one hop, doesn't move the argmax. Pairs naturally with SuperFlux (feed flux directly into the comb).

05Beat-tracking algorithms — beats.py

Comb-filter aligner (Böck-Schedl 2011) ⭐ port candidate

BeatTrackingProcessor (beats.py:385-525), detect_beats (beats.py:301-381).

  1. Smooth activation with Hamming over 0.09–0.14 s.
  2. Estimate dominant interval τ via comb-filter histogram (above).
  3. For each candidate start phase φ ∈ [0, τ): score Σ_n a[φ + n·τ] · w(offset) where w is a Hamming centered on the expected position; argmax to snap; recurse at position + interval.
  4. Pick the φ with highest total score; emit beat sequence.

Look-ahead variant (beats.py:494-520) re-estimates interval in a ±10 s window per beat — allows tempo drift. Constants: LOOK_ASIDE=0.2, LOOK_AHEAD=10 s (beats.py:450-451).

In pure-math terms: template alignment. Given τ from the tempogram, try all phases, score each with a Hamming-windowed sum over expected positions, pick the best. This is what we want for phase-locked beat prediction.

CRF beat tracker — beats_crf.pyx

  • Initial dist (beats_crf.pyx:29-51): uniform over first τ frames.
  • Transition dist (beats_crf.pyx:54-81): norm.pdf(log2(j), loc=log2(τ), scale=INTERVAL_SIGMA) for j ∈ [0, 2τ). INTERVAL_SIGMA=0.18 in log2 units ≈ ±18% tempo wiggle per beat.
  • Viterbi (beats_crf.pyx:146-242): max over v_p[i−j] + transition[j]; backtrack from final argmax.

Tractable online (length × τ ≈ length × 60). But you only get one tempo per call (picked up front). The comb-aligner above is simpler and produces equivalent output for our purposes.

06DBN state space — beats_hmm.py

BeatStateSpace (beats_hmm.py:23-99): for each modeled interval τ (in frames, integer), allocate τ states with state_positions = linspace(0, 1, τ, endpoint=False). Total states = Σ τ over the tempo grid.

At min_bpm=55, max_bpm=215, fps=100: τ ∈ [28, 109], ~5500 states naive; num_tempi=60 log-spaced caps it at ~3500.

Transition (beats_hmm.py:272-325): within a beat, state[t+1] = state[t]+1; at boundary, prob ∝ exp(−λ · |τ_to/τ_from − 1|) with TRANSITION_LAMBDA=100 (beats.py:948). Higher λ ⇒ stickier tempo.

Observation (beats_hmm.py:513-573): each state is beat if state_position < 1/OBSERVATION_LAMBDA (first 6.25% of beat, with OBSERVATION_LAMBDA=16), non-beat otherwise. Densities are log(a[t]) for beat, log((1 − a[t]) / (λ − 1)) for non-beat. The (λ−1) normalisation makes the two compete fairly across 15/16 non-beat states.

Tractability for us: ~3500 states × 100 Hz × ~10 sparse transitions ≈ 2 Mops/s. Doable in pure numpy. But the DBN's killer feature — joint inference of tempo and phase from a noisy continuous-valued activation — only matters if you have a high-quality activation in the first place. Feeding a sparse 0/1 onset stream into the DBN works but loses most of the dynamic information that makes it beat the "predict at last+IOI" baseline. Valuable only if SuperFlux is in first.

07Downbeat detection — downbeats.py

Verdict: model-driven only. There is no signal-level "kick on the 1" heuristic anywhere in madmom. Downbeat tracking requires the separately-trained RNNDownBeatProcessor (downbeats.py:30-95) outputting a 2-column activation. The DBN then has 2–4 beat bar state spaces (BarStateSpace, beats_hmm.py:102-169) and a third observation column (RNNDownBeatTrackingObservationModel, beats_hmm.py:576-638).

No low-hanging fruit here. If we want downbeat without an RNN, the classic heuristic (which madmom doesn't use) is low-band energy correlation at beat positions: cumulate low_energy[beat_k] · cos(2πk/N) for N ∈ {2, 3, 4}, pick N + phase with maximum response. Coarse but useful — leaves us with a /audio/beat/count ∈ {1..N}.

09Prioritized port plan

1. SuperFlux ODF on FFT binsAdopt~2 hours · highest value

Canonical realtime ODF Alloc-free Study: onsets.py:196-240

Port: ~30 lines into a new server/dsp/flux.py (or extend onset.py). Keep one previous-hop copy of the FFTPostProcessor's bin array; per hop compute prev_max = maximum_filter1d(prev, size=3, mode='reflect'), flux = max(0, cur − prev_max).sum(). Feed flux into the existing Schmitt-trigger infrastructure (per-band by partitioning bin range into low/mid/high sub-ranges, summed independently).

Why: Replaces 3-band coarse novelty with the canonical realtime ODF. Tightens kick/snare/hat separation, especially when L/M/H edges aren't well-aligned for the genre. Hop rate (~100 Hz) instead of block rate (~187 Hz) is fine for beat tracking; keep the per-band L/M/H Schmitt for downstream consumers that need block-rate triggers.

2. Comb-filter tempo estimatorAdopt~1 day · high value

Robust to missed onsets ~7 kops at 100 Hz Study: tempo.py:109-163, 375-418, 198-255

Port: ~80 lines. Online comb-filter bank — maintain _comb_buffer[max_tau+1, n_taus] (~100 × 60 × float64 ≈ 50 KB), per hop do y = a + α · _comb_buffer[−intervals]; argmax-pool into _hist_buffer[10s, n_taus] ring; every 1 s compute argmax(sum(hist, axis=0)) → BPM.

Why: Replaces median-of-12-IOIs, which is brittle to missed/spurious onsets. Pairs naturally with #1 — feeding SuperFlux directly into the comb is the textbook approach. Same machinery later gives us per-tempo confidence (the histogram), which lets the BPM decay-to-0 logic become a confidence threshold instead of a 5 s timeout.

3. Phase-locked beat alignerAdopt~½ day on top of #2

Predictive beat times OSC: phase + next_beat Study: beats.py:301-381, 450-451

Port: ~50 lines. Given tempo τ from #2, maintain phase φ ∈ [0, τ). Once per hop: if flux[t] > threshold, snap φ ← (t mod τ) weighted by a Hamming around the expected position (exactly detect_beats's windowed argmax); else let φ advance. Predicted next beat = t + ((φ − (t mod τ)) mod τ). Fire when crossed.

Why: Closes the "beat prediction" item. Adds /audio/beat/phase ∈ [0,1) and /audio/beat/next_t — downstream visuals can render anticipatory between-beat motion (ramps that hit zero on the kick, beat-quantised cues). High-payoff for vibe-coding / VJ use.

4. Falling-edge fire for Schmitt-triggerAdopt15 min · trivial

Removes 1-2 frame jitter Study: onsets.py:942-956

Port: ~5 lines. Track nov_prev[i]; fire on state == armed AND nov[i] < nov_prev[i]. Aligns onset time with the actual peak instead of the threshold crossing.

Why: Free upgrade. Tightens onset timing accuracy without changing detection rate. Helps #3's phase-snap because each snap is anchored on the true peak.

5. Mini-DBN beat trackerLong-term~3 days

Only valuable after #1-#3 Study: beats_hmm.py:23-99, 221-325, 513-573; beats.py:1064-1142

Port: ~300 lines. Build BeatStateSpace with ~60 log-spaced tempi over [60, 180] BPM at 100 Hz, sparse exponential transition (λ=100), 1/16-of-beat observation model. Online forward: α[t+1] = obs · (T · α[t]). Argmax state → (tempo, phase).

Why / why-not: Strictly more robust than #3 — handles tempo drift gracefully, never has to "pick" between octave candidates because both states stay alive in the posterior. But only shines with a smooth-valued continuous activation (#1's output). Skip until #1-#4 prove insufficient.

6. Downbeat counter via low-band emphasis heuristicConsider~½ day

Not in madmom — heuristic only

Port: cumulate low_energy[beat_k] · cos(2πk/N) for N ∈ {2,3,4}, pick the (N, phase) with maximum response. Emit /audio/beat/count ∈ {1..N}. Coarse, no model, but instantly useful for visuals.

madmom does this with an RNN trained on labels — we can't replicate that without the model. The heuristic is a pragmatic substitute. Only worthwhile after #3 because it needs phase-aligned beat times.

10TL;DR

The 2018 outer wrapper is mostly anti-reference (mp scaffolding, allocation in callbacks, 10 s latency). The actual algorithmic gold is in madmom/features/:

  1. SuperFlux on our FFT bins → canonical realtime ODF, ~30 lines, immediate kick/snare/hat separation upgrade.
  2. Comb-filter tempo → replaces median-of-IOIs, robust to missed onsets, ~80 lines.
  3. Phase-locked aligner → predictive /audio/beat/phase + /audio/next_beat_in, ~50 lines.
  4. Falling-edge Schmitt fire → 5-line accuracy upgrade.

Together: ~6-8 hours, ~165 lines of new code. Gets us to feature parity with madmom's classical (non-NN) pipeline on our own realtime infrastructure. The DBN and CRF beat trackers are real options afterwards, but only meaningful once we have SuperFlux feeding them.
